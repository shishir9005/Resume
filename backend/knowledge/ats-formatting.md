# ATS-Friendly Formatting

## How an ATS reads a resume
An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) converts your file to plain text, then splits it into sections such as Experience, Education, and Skills. Recruiters then search that text for keywords. Anything the parser cannot turn into clean text is effectively invisible: text inside images, text boxes, headers and footers, and complex tables are the most common problems. If a recruiter cannot find you by keyword, a human never sees the resume.

## Layout rules that parse reliably
Use a single-column layout. Two-column templates often get read across both columns line by line, mixing your skills into your job descriptions. Use standard section headings: "Experience", "Education", "Projects", "Skills". Creative headings like "Where I've Been" may not be recognized. Keep dates in one consistent format, for example "Jun 2025 – Aug 2025", aligned on the same line as the role.

## Fonts, symbols and file type
Use a common font such as Arial, Calibri, or Helvetica at 10–12 pt. Avoid decorative characters, box-drawing characters, icons for phone or email, and skill bars or star ratings; parsers turn them into garbage characters or drop them. Submit a PDF generated from a text editor (not a scanned image) unless the job posting asks for DOCX. Name the file clearly, for example "Firstname_Lastname_Resume.pdf".

## Personal information and privacy
An ATS does not need your full street address, ZIP code, date of birth, or ID numbers. "City, Country" is enough. Include email, phone, and links to LinkedIn and GitHub as plain text so they can be parsed and clicked. Removing a detailed address also reduces privacy risk if the resume is shared widely.

## Length
For students and early-career candidates, one page is the standard. Two pages are acceptable only with substantial relevant experience. Cut older or unrelated items before shrinking the font below 10 pt or removing whitespace entirely.
