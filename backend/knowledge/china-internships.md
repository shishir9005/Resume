# Applying for Internships in China

## Recruitment seasons
Large Chinese tech companies recruit students in cycles. Summer internship (暑期实习) applications usually open in spring, roughly February to April, and strong summer interns often receive return offers. Autumn campus recruitment (秋招) for full-time graduate roles runs roughly August to November, with a smaller spring round (春招). Daily internships (日常实习) are open year-round and are a good entry point. Dates vary by company, so check each company's campus recruitment (校园招聘) site early.

## Where to find openings
Company campus recruitment websites and their WeChat official accounts are the primary channels for big companies. Common platforms include BOSS Zhipin (BOSS直聘), Shixiseng (实习僧) for internships, Nowcoder (牛客网) for tech roles and interview preparation, Liepin, and LinkedIn for multinational companies. Referrals (内推) from current employees are widely used and can speed up screening.

## The hiring process
A typical tech internship process is: online application → online written test or coding assessment (笔试, often algorithm problems) → two or three technical interviews → an HR interview. Prepare data structures and algorithms practice alongside your resume, and be ready to explain every project in detail, including design choices and what you would improve.

## Resume conventions
Chinese companies often accept both Chinese and English resumes; for international students an English resume is usually fine at multinational and large tech companies, and a Chinese version helps for domestic companies. Chinese-style resumes sometimes include a photo and more personal details, but technical content still matters most: projects, internships, and skills. Keep the same one-page, results-focused style. List language ability clearly, for example "English (fluent), Chinese (HSK 5)".

## Notes for international students
International students should confirm internship eligibility and any required permissions with their university's international student office before accepting an offer, because rules about internships and residence permits can change and may require a university-approved annotation. Showing Chinese language ability, even intermediate, is a real advantage in many teams.
