# The Professional Summary

## Do you need a summary?
A short summary at the top helps a recruiter understand you in five seconds and is a natural place for keywords. For students it is optional but useful when your target role is not obvious from your degree, for example a mechanical engineering student applying for data analyst internships. The ATS Resume Scorer gives a small formatting credit when a summary is present.

## How to write it
Two or three lines, no more. Structure: who you are + your strongest relevant skills + what you are looking for. Example: "Computer science junior focused on backend development. Built and deployed a FastAPI + Supabase web app used by 200+ students. Seeking a summer 2026 backend engineering internship."

## What to avoid
Avoid first-person pronouns ("I am a passionate..."), vague adjectives ("hard-working, motivated team player"), and objective statements about what you want to gain ("seeking an opportunity to grow"). Focus on what you offer. Do not repeat the entire skills list.

## Tailoring the summary
Change the target role and the two or three highlighted skills for each application. This is the fastest place to align your resume with a specific job description without rewriting every bullet.
